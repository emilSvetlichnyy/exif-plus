Remove photo location before selling online

Photos taken at home can hold the exact GPS coordinates of your home. Clean them before you post a listing.

Updated 2026-11-06

Direct answer: Before you post listing photos, remove their GPS data. Marketplaces often re-encode photos, but you cannot verify that for every listing route, and photos you send to buyers by message or email can still carry location. Strip the metadata first.

Steps on iPhone

  1. Open EXIF+ and select the listing photo.
  2. Remove location (or all metadata) and save. The cleaned photo is added as a new item; your original stays.
  3. For many photos, use batch remove (Premium), or clean them one by one.
  4. Upload the cleaned copies.

Full walkthrough: remove location from photos on iPhone.

Steps on a computer

  1. Open the GPS remover or strip EXIF online.
  2. Choose a JPEG, PNG or WebP and download the cleaned file.

Also check the picture

Metadata is one risk. Visible house numbers, street views through windows and parcels with your address are another. See can a photo reveal your location.

FAQ

Do Facebook Marketplace photos show my location?

The public copy is commonly re-encoded, but not guaranteed. Remove GPS before uploading and before sending photos to buyers.

How do I remove location from photos before selling?

Strip GPS from each photo with a metadata remover, then upload the cleaned copies.

Can buyers see where I live from item photos?

If the file keeps GPS data, yes. Also check for visible clues in the image.

Do I need to remove all metadata or only location?

Location matters most. Removing everything also hides camera and date info.
