Best app to remove EXIF data on iPhone: 5 apps compared

Five iPhone apps that remove photo metadata, compared on what they actually do. We make one of them (EXIF+), so we say where the others are better.

Updated 2026-11-12

Direct answer: All five apps below remove GPS and EXIF on the device without uploading your photo. Pick Metapho if you also need videos, RAW files or a Mac; Exif Metadata if you want the most-reviewed editor with a Photos extension; Photo Metadata EXIF Remover if you only want to share clean copies of photos and videos; and EXIF+ if you want view, edit and remove in one free app that always saves a new copy and keeps the original.

Disclosure: EXIF+ is our app. Facts about other apps come from their US App Store listings as of 12 Nov 2026; features and prices change, so check the listing before you buy. We have not run a lab test of every app.

Where each one wins

  • Metapho — the widest format support: HEIC, DNG and other RAW files, Live Photos and videos, files in the Files app and on SMB or external drives, and the same purchase on Mac. It can also replace the original instead of saving a copy.
  • Exif Metadata — the largest number of reviews in this group, a Photos extension to see metadata without leaving Photos, and filters for photos missing location or metadata (Pro).
  • Photo Metadata EXIF Remover — the simplest privacy tool: share or save a copy with no metadata, including videos, from the Share button in Photos. It also flags AI and Content Credentials (C2PA) info. It does not edit fields.
  • EXIF Remover – Photo Secure — handles photos and videos and can strip a video's audio; shows the place on a map before you post.
  • EXIF+ — view, edit and remove in one app, free for single photos; every change is saved as a new photo so the original stays; History keeps 50 before/after entries. It does not handle video, RAW or XMP, and has no Photos-app extension.

Five criteria that matter

  1. Processing stays on the device. All five say photos are not sent to a server.
  2. Original is kept. A saved copy means a mistake cannot destroy your only photo. Metapho lets you choose; the others save copies.
  3. HEIC works. iPhone photos are usually HEIC; many browser tools cannot open them.
  4. Removes more than location. Camera model, software and date also describe you.
  5. Batch. Cleaning one photo at a time stops being practical after a few.

No app at all

The iOS share sheet's Options → Location drops location for one send, but leaves camera and date tags and gives you no cleaned file. For a single JPEG, PNG or WebP on any device, the browser EXIF remover works without installing anything. On a computer, ExifTool removes almost any tag from many file types from the command line.

When EXIF+ fits, and when it does not

It fits if you have an iPhone or iPad (iOS 17+) and want to see, edit or remove EXIF, IPTC and GPS on photos in your Photo Library. Single-photo view, edit and remove are free; multi-select batch and strip-on-share need Premium. Cleaned photos are saved as new Library items and the original is kept.

It does not fit if you need to edit video metadata, read or write XMP, or work on a computer. For one JPEG, PNG or WebP on a computer, the browser EXIF remover is enough.

FAQ

What is the best app to remove EXIF data on iPhone?

It depends on the job. Metapho covers the most formats including video and RAW; Exif Metadata is the most reviewed editor; Photo Metadata EXIF Remover is the simplest share-a-clean-copy tool; EXIF+ combines view, edit and remove for free on single photos and always keeps the original.

Is there a free app to remove metadata from iPhone photos?

Yes. Photo Metadata EXIF Remover is free, Photo Secure is free with ads, Exif Metadata is free to try, and EXIF+ is free for single photos. Batch features usually cost extra.

Which apps remove metadata from videos?

Metapho, Photo Metadata EXIF Remover and Photo Secure list video support. EXIF+ works with photos only.

Can I remove EXIF on iPhone without an app?

Partly. The share sheet's Options lets you turn off location for that send, but it does not remove camera and date tags from a saved file.

Do these apps upload my photos?

All five state that photos are processed on the device and not sent to a server. Apps that show a map may send coordinates to Apple Maps to draw it.
