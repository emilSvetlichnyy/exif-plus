iPhone share-sheet Options vs an EXIF remover app

The share sheet can drop location for one send. An EXIF app changes the file itself. Which one you need depends on where the photo goes.

Updated 2026-11-06

Direct answer: If you only need to send one photo through Messages, Mail or AirDrop without its location, the share sheet's Options switch is enough. If you need the photo cleaned of camera, date and GPS tags, kept as its own file, or cleaned in bulk, use an app like EXIF+.

Practical rule

Casual send to a friend: Options. Upload to a website, marketplace or social app, or anything public: clean the file first. See what iMessage and AirDrop do with location and share without metadata.

When EXIF+ fits, and when it does not

It fits if you have an iPhone or iPad (iOS 17+) and want to see, edit or remove EXIF, IPTC and GPS on photos in your Photo Library. Single-photo view, edit and remove are free; multi-select batch and strip-on-share need Premium. Cleaned photos are saved as new Library items and the original is kept.

It does not fit if you need to edit video metadata, read or write XMP, or work on a computer. For one JPEG, PNG or WebP on a computer, the browser EXIF remover is enough.

FAQ

How do I send a photo without location on iPhone?

Open the share sheet, tap Options at the top, and turn Location off before sending.

Does the Options switch change the saved photo?

No. It only changes what is sent in that share.

Does the share sheet remove camera and date info?

Do not assume so. The location switch targets location; use a metadata remover for the rest.

When should I use an EXIF app instead?

When the photo goes to a public site, when you want a reusable clean copy, or when you have many photos.

Need this on iPhone?

EXIF+ views, edits, and removes EXIF/IPTC metadata — including GPS — on-device. Single-photo clean and edit are available to start; Premium unlocks multi-select batch tools and share-without-metadata. Cleaned photos are saved as new Photo Library items (originals stay).
