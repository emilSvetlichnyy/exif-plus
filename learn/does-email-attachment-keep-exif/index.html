Do email attachments keep EXIF data?

Usually yes. An emailed photo tends to arrive as the same file, so the recipient can read its EXIF and location.

Updated 2026-11-12

Direct answer: A photo attached to an email is normally delivered as a file, so its EXIF and GPS data travel with it. Some mail apps can resize an image before sending, which may rewrite the file, but that is not a reliable way to remove metadata. Clean the photo before you attach it.

Why email is different from social apps

Social apps re-encode images they host, which often drops metadata from the public copy. Email carries your file to the recipient as is. The same is true for files sent as documents in chat apps.

How to send a photo by email without location

  1. Remove location or all metadata first: GPS remover, strip EXIF online, or EXIF+ on iPhone.
  2. Attach the cleaned copy, not the original.
  3. Open the cleaned file in the image metadata viewer if you want to confirm.

On iPhone, the share sheet's Options switch can also remove location for a single send; see Options vs an EXIF app.

FAQ

Does Gmail remove EXIF data?

Do not rely on it. An attached photo generally arrives as the file you sent, with its metadata.

Can a person who receives my email photo see my location?

Yes, if the attached file contains GPS data.

How do I email a photo without metadata?

Strip the metadata from the file first, then attach the cleaned copy.

Does resizing a photo in Mail remove metadata?

It may change the file, but do not count on it. Remove metadata explicitly.
