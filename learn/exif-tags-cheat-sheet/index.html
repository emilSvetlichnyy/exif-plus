EXIF tags cheat sheet

The EXIF fields you will meet most often in a photo, what each one means, and which ones can identify you or your location.

Updated 2026-11-19

Direct answer: EXIF (Exchangeable image file format) is a block of tags a camera writes into a photo file. The ones that matter for privacy are the GPS tags (where), the date tags (when) and Make, Model and Software (what device). Exposure tags such as ISO and FNumber describe the shot and are low risk.

Privacy-sensitive tags

TagWhat it holdsRisk
GPSLatitude, GPSLongitudeCoordinates where the photo was takenHigh: can point to a home, school or workplace
GPSAltitudeHeight above or below sea levelMedium: narrows a location
GPSImgDirectionCompass direction the camera facedMedium
DateTimeOriginalWhen the shutter was pressedMedium: reveals routines and absence from home
Make, ModelPhone or camera brand and modelLow to medium: identifies your device
SoftwareFirmware or editing app versionLow
BodySerialNumber, LensSerialNumberSerial numbers (mostly dedicated cameras)High: can link photos to one camera
Artist, CopyrightName and rights text, if the camera or an app wrote themDepends: can name you

Shot-setting tags (low risk)

TagWhat it holds
ExposureTimeShutter speed, for example 1/120 s
FNumberAperture, for example f/1.8
ISOSensor sensitivity
FocalLengthLens focal length in millimetres
FlashWhether the flash fired
OrientationHow to rotate the image for display
ColorSpaceColour profile flag, such as sRGB

What to do with them

Keep shot settings if you share photos for learning. Remove GPS and serial numbers before you post publicly. EXIF+ lists EXIF, IPTC and GPS tags for photos in your library and can remove all of them, or only location. In a browser, use the EXIF viewer or the GPS remover.

FAQ

What are the most common EXIF tags?

Make, Model, DateTimeOriginal, ExposureTime, FNumber, ISO, FocalLength, Orientation and the GPS tags are the ones found in most phone and camera photos.

Which EXIF tags are a privacy risk?

GPS latitude and longitude first, then the date and time, device Make and Model, and camera serial numbers.

Does every photo have EXIF?

No. Screenshots usually have little or none, and many websites and messengers strip it from uploaded copies.

Can I remove only GPS and keep the rest?

Yes. EXIF+ and the browser GPS remover can remove location only, or all metadata.
